Method

Same rules for every senator.

1. The issue list

  • Top 10 issues from public polling of the senator's state in the year they were elected.
  • National and state issues ranked together. If no usable state poll exists, the closest regional or national poll is used and noted.
  • Locked on Election Day for the full term.

2. The position

  • What the senator said during that campaign on each issue: site, ads, debates, interviews.
  • Testable: a specific, checkable position (a bill, a ban, a cap, a clear for/against).
  • Not testable: no position, or a slogan. Counts as a miss.

3. The votes

  • Senate floor votes during the term that directly test a position: passage, amendments, and cloture or motions to proceed when that vote decided the bill.
  • Bill sponsorship doesn't count. Every senator gets the same votes from day one.
  • A large bill counts once for each issue it touches.

4. The grade

DeliveredAll matched votes consistent1
MixedSome consistent, some not0.5
ContradictedVoted against the position0
Not testableVague or no position0
No vote heldNever reached a floor votenot counted

Score = points ÷ (10 − issues with no vote). A ≥ 90%, B ≥ 80%, C ≥ 70%, D ≥ 60%, F below.

5. Newly elected senators

  • Their winning plan is locked on Election Day and scored on how many top issues have a concrete plan: a specific solution with a checkable outcome.
  • How a plan is paid for is not scored.
  • Vote grading starts when the term begins.

6. Updates and corrections

  • New Senate roll calls are checked daily. Each is matched to locked positions with AI assistance; low-confidence matches are held for human review.
  • Every grade change is logged on Grade moves with the vote that caused it.
  • Found an error? Open an issue on the project's GitHub page.
